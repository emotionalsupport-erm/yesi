Maomao

Raised in the pleasure district by an apothecary who taught her to read poisons like other children read picture books, Maomao never asked to be sold into the rear palace. She'd rather be grinding herbs than curtseying to nobles — but a mysterious illness among the emperor's infant children draws out the one skill she can't hide: she notices what everyone else misses.

Sharp-tongued, quietly brilliant, and allergic to being praised for anything but her work, she treats the court's endless schemes the way she treats a locked medicine cabinet: a puzzle worth picking apart, whether or not anyone asked her to.

The Rear Palace

Perfume thick enough to taste, silk enough to bury a body in — the rear palace houses hundreds of consorts competing for an emperor's attention they may never receive. Beneath the beauty runs a current of jealousy, rumor, and the occasional attempted murder, all of it dressed up as etiquette.

Jinshi

Impossibly beautiful and fully aware of it, the eunuch official Jinshi oversees the rear palace's daily affairs with a smile that unsettles everyone except Maomao, who finds his looks about as useful as a decorative jar — nice enough, but she'd still rather know what's inside.

The Quack Doctor

Round, gentle, and about as intimidating as a steamed bun, the palace physician nicknamed "the quack" is far less foolish than his bumbling manner suggests. Maomao's unofficial mentor, he lets her raid his medicine chest in exchange for doing most of the actual diagnosing.

Case Notes

The Silvering Sickness

Two infant princes fall ill in succession, their symptoms dismissed as a curse by everyone but the new food taster, who recognizes the slow signature of lead in the palace's imported cosmetics before it claims a third child.

The Untouched Seal

A jar of tribute wine arrives for a favored consort, its jade seal unbroken and its cork untouched — proof enough for the guards at the door. Maomao finds the truth beneath the wax: a needle-thin puncture, sealed shut again before anyone thought to look underneath.

The Untouched Cup

A rival consort's tea is found poisoned before it's even served — proof, Maomao argues, that the real target was never meant to drink it at all.
